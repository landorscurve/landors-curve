The Acceleration of Deskilling

The skill floor keeps moving. As tools improve, the time people have to adapt gets shorter. Jobs do not always disappear overnight. More often, they absorb more skills, more tools, and more responsibilities until the old version of the job no longer exists.

Every new tool changes what the job requires.

What is deskilling?
Deskilling is when technology simplifies parts of a job while raising the total expectations placed on the worker. The tool helps, but the role expands.

    The Framework
    The Three Rules of Landor's Curve

    Each rule describes a pattern that has repeated across every major technological shift in history.

    Rule 1 — Acceleration
    Each wave changes work faster than the last.
    Swordsmiths had generations to adapt. Typists had decades. Today, the shift can happen within a few years of a tool entering the market.
    Rule 2 — Rising Skill Floor
    Routine tasks are absorbed. Expectations move higher.
    Technology handles the repetitive work. In return, the job absorbs new responsibilities. The floor of what the role requires keeps rising.
    Rule 3 — Mastery or Operation
    The most stable paths are mastery or operation.
    Workers who deepen their craft beyond what tools can replicate, or who learn to operate the tools themselves, tend to hold the most durable positions.
