Transparency

OUR SOURCES
& TRANSPARENCY

Trust is everything. Landor's Curve is built to show where its information comes from, how that information is used, and what users should understand before making decisions.

Section 1
Why This Page Exists

Better career insight should not be reserved only for institutions with large budgets. Landor's Curve brings together public workforce data, published research, current signals, and structured analysis so individuals can ask better questions about work, education, risk, opportunity, and career change.

The goal is not to overwhelm users with raw data. The goal is to make complex information easier to understand, compare, and apply.

Section 2
The Landor's Curve Analysis Engine

Landor's Curve does not rely on one source or one type of analysis. The system combines several layers of information to generate directional career insights.

Those layers may include official workforce data, published labor-market research, occupational and education data, user-provided resumes or job descriptions, current job-market signals when available, founder-developed frameworks, and AI-assisted synthesis.

Section 3
Source Layers

Each tool draws from different combinations of these four layers depending on the type of analysis being performed.

Layer 1
Official Workforce Data
Used for occupational tasks, skills, wages, employment outlook, training requirements, and labor-market baselines.
O*NET BLS Public occupational databases
Layer 2
Published Research and Reports
Used for broader workforce trends, AI exposure, automation pressure, industry shifts, and long-term labor-market interpretation.
Brookings McKinsey Goldman Sachs WEF OECD
Layer 3
Current Signals
Used for recent job-market patterns, employer language, emerging skill demand, and role descriptions. Especially relevant in deeper reports.
These signals are not meant to replace official data. They help show what may be changing right now, before official data catches up.
Layer 4
User Inputs
Used to personalize analysis. Resumes, job descriptions, background descriptions, and career goals are processed only for the user's analysis and are not stored permanently.
Your information shapes your result. It is not sold, shared, or used to train models without explicit consent.
Source Details — Layer 1 and Layer 2
O*NET
Occupational Information Network — US Dept. of Labor
Reviewed: Rolling updates, 2024–2025
The most granular job-level dataset available. Breaks down specific tasks, skills, knowledge, and work activities for each occupation.
Strength
Extremely detailed task-level data. Shows what a job actually requires day to day.
Limitation
Updated on a rolling cycle. Very new roles may lag behind reality.
BLS
Bureau of Labor Statistics — US Dept. of Labor
Reviewed: 2024–2025 Occupational Outlook Handbook
Official US government source for employment data, wage statistics, and ten-year job outlook projections.
Strength
Authoritative. The gold standard for employment numbers and long-range projections.
Limitation
Conservative by design. Measures what has already happened. Often underestimates disruption speed.
Brookings
Brookings Institution
Reviewed: 2023–2024 automation and AI research
Independent policy research on automation exposure, wage polarization, and workforce transitions.
Strength
Strong on automation risk. One of the most cited independent sources on AI and the future of work.
Limitation
Research-focused. Not always translated into practical individual career guidance.
McKinsey
McKinsey Global Institute
Reviewed: 2024 The State of AI report
Major annual reports on automation, workforce transitions, and the economics of AI adoption globally.
Strength
Deep, well-resourced research. Strong economic modeling of disruption at scale.
Limitation
Produced by a consulting firm with clients in affected industries. Read with that context in mind.
Goldman Sachs
Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research
Reviewed: 2023–2024 AI and labor market reports
Economic research on automation, AI displacement, and labor market transformation.
Strength
Strong quantitative modeling on which job categories are exposed to generative AI.
Limitation
Written for investors. Framing can differ from what is useful for individual career decisions.
WEF
World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs Report
Reviewed: Future of Jobs Report 2025
Annual report surveying employers globally on which roles are growing, shrinking, and transforming.
Strength
International scope. One of the few sources capturing global workforce trends.
Limitation
Based on employer surveys. Reflects what employers believe will happen, not necessarily what will.
LinkedIn
LinkedIn Workforce Confidence Reports & Jobs on the Rise
Source: Publicly available quarterly reports
Publicly available quarterly and annual reports based on real hiring trends, skill demand, and workforce movement.
Strength
Real hiring signal. Shows what employers are actually posting and paying for right now.
Limitation
Skews toward white-collar fields. Less representative of trades and blue-collar work.
OECD
OECD Employment Outlook
Reviewed: OECD Employment Outlook 2024
Annual workforce analysis covering 38 member countries on automation, inequality, and labor market transitions.
Strength
Rigorous, independent, internationally comparative.
Limitation
Policy-focused. Can feel abstract relative to on-the-ground career decisions.
Section 4
How AI Is Used
What AI Does Here

AI helps synthesize, compare, and explain information from the source layers above. It is used to generate career analysis, roadmap suggestions, resume reframing, and opportunity identification based on the inputs you provide.

AI does not make decisions for you. It helps you see patterns, comparisons, and options more quickly than manual research would allow. You decide what to do with the output.

AI can be incomplete or imperfect. It can reflect patterns in the data it was trained on. Results should be treated as a starting point for research, reflection, and conversation — not as a final answer.

For important decisions — career transitions, educational investments, financial planning, legal matters — verify the output with qualified professionals and additional sources.

Section 5
Updates and Currency
How Fresh Is the Data?

Landor's Curve uses a mix of official government data, published research, AI-assisted synthesis, and current signals. Each has a different update cycle.

Official data like O*NET and BLS updates on a rolling annual cycle. Published research from organizations like Brookings, McKinsey, and WEF is reviewed and incorporated as new reports are released. Current signals may reflect more recent patterns when available in deeper reports.

Where possible, source review dates are shown in the source detail cards above. The tools are designed to improve over time as sources are refreshed and methods are refined.

Section 6
What This Website Is
What It Is

A planning and reflection tool for understanding career change, automation pressure, skill transfer, adjacent opportunities, and possible next steps.

Designed to help people ask better questions before investing time, money, and energy into a path.

What It Is Not

It is not a guarantee of employment, an official labor forecast, or a replacement for professional career, financial, legal, or educational advice.

It is not affiliated with or sponsored by any school, training program, employer, or institution.

Section 7
What Sets It Apart

Most career tools are built to funnel people toward a product, school, job board, or paid service. Landor's Curve is built differently.

🔓
No hidden agenda. The tools are not sponsored by schools, training programs, or employers trying to steer users toward a particular outcome. The goal is honest analysis, not sales.
📊
Multiple source layers. Most career tools use one or two data sources. Landor's Curve combines official government data, published research, current signals, and AI-assisted synthesis to give a more complete picture.
⚖️
Strengths and limitations shown. Every major source used by Landor's Curve has both strengths and limitations listed openly on this page. No source is perfect. Users deserve to know that.
🆓
Free tools that are genuinely useful. The free tools are not empty demos designed to force an upgrade. They are built to give real insight. Paid reports exist because deeper analysis, larger searches, and document generation require real resources.
🔒
Your data stays yours. Resumes and job descriptions uploaded to the tools are used only for your analysis. They are not stored permanently, sold, or used to train AI models without explicit consent.
Section 8
What This Website Is Not
Important Note

Landor's Curve and PathDecider provide directional career analysis, planning tools, and founder-developed frameworks based on public workforce data, published research, and AI-assisted comparison.

They are not guarantees of employment, official labor forecasts, or substitutes for professional, financial, legal, educational, or career counseling.

The goal is to help users make more informed decisions, not to tell them what they must do. Important decisions should involve multiple sources, trusted advisors, and personal judgment.
