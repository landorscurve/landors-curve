SOURCES
& METHOD

Landor's Curve combines public workforce data, published labor-market research, occupational databases, and AI-assisted analysis to generate directional career insights. The tools are designed to help users ask better questions, compare options, and plan next steps.

What This Website Is and Is Not
What It Is

A planning and reflection tool for understanding career change, automation pressure, skill transfer, adjacent opportunities, and possible next steps. Designed to help people ask better questions before investing time, money, and energy.

What It Is Not

It is not a guarantee of employment, an official labor forecast, or a replacement for professional career, financial, legal, or educational advice. It is not affiliated with or sponsored by any school, training program, employer, or institution.

Where the Data Comes From
Data Sources

Every analysis on this site draws from the following publicly available research. Each one has real strengths and real limitations. Both are listed honestly.

O*NET
Occupational Information Network — US Dept. of Labor
Last reviewed: Rolling updates, 2024–2025
The most granular job-level dataset available. Breaks down specific tasks, skills, knowledge, and work activities for each occupation.
Strength
Extremely detailed task-level data. Closest thing to an x-ray of what a job requires day to day.
Limitation
Updated on a rolling cycle. Rapidly evolving roles may lag behind reality.
BLS
Bureau of Labor Statistics — US Dept. of Labor
Last reviewed: 2024–2025 Occupational Outlook Handbook
Official US government source for employment data, wage statistics, and ten-year job outlook projections.
Strength
Authoritative. The gold standard for employment numbers and long-range projections.
Limitation
Conservative by design. Measures what has already happened. Often underestimates disruption speed.
Brookings
Brookings Institution
Last reviewed: 2023–2024 automation and AI research
Independent policy research on automation exposure, wage polarization, and workforce transitions.
Strength
Strong on automation risk and displacement analysis. One of the most cited independent sources on AI and the future of work.
Limitation
Research-focused. Not always translated into practical individual career guidance.
McKinsey
McKinsey Global Institute
Last reviewed: 2024 The State of AI report
Major annual reports on automation, workforce transitions, and the economics of AI adoption globally.
Strength
Deep, well-resourced research with global scope. Strong economic modeling of disruption at scale.
Limitation
Produced by a consulting firm with clients in affected industries. Read with that context in mind.
Goldman Sachs
Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research
Last reviewed: 2023–2024 AI and labor market reports
Economic research on automation, AI displacement, and labor market transformation.
Strength
Strong quantitative modeling on which job categories are exposed to generative AI.
Limitation
Written for investors. Framing can differ from what is useful for individual career decisions.
WEF
World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs Report
Last reviewed: Future of Jobs Report 2025
Annual report surveying employers globally on which roles are growing, shrinking, and transforming.
Strength
International scope. One of the few sources capturing global workforce trends.
Limitation
Based on employer surveys. Reflects what employers believe will happen, not necessarily what will.
LinkedIn
LinkedIn Workforce Confidence Reports and Jobs on the Rise
Source: Publicly available quarterly reports
Publicly available quarterly and annual reports based on real hiring trends, skill demand, and workforce movement.
Strength
Real hiring signal. Shows what employers are actually posting and paying for right now.
Limitation
Skews toward white-collar and professional fields. Less representative of trades and blue-collar work.
OECD
OECD Employment Outlook
Last reviewed: OECD Employment Outlook 2024
Annual workforce analysis covering 38 member countries on automation, inequality, and labor market transitions.
Strength
Rigorous, independent, internationally comparative. Strong on policy implications of disruption.
Limitation
Policy-focused. Can feel abstract relative to on-the-ground career decisions.
How AI Is Used
AI and Limitations

AI helps synthesize, compare, and explain information from the sources listed above. It is used to generate career analysis, roadmap suggestions, resume reframing, and opportunity identification based on the inputs you provide.

AI can be incomplete or imperfect. It can reflect biases in the data it was trained on. Users should verify important decisions with multiple sources and qualified professionals when appropriate.

The tools do not make decisions for you. They help you see more clearly so you can make better decisions for yourself.

Data Freshness
Updates and Currency

Landor's Curve uses a mix of public workforce data, published labor-market research, manually reviewed sources, and AI-assisted analysis. Some information comes from structured datasets updated periodically. Some comes from reports or research updated less often.

The tools are designed to improve over time as sources are refreshed, methods are refined, and more current analysis becomes available. Where possible, source review dates are shown on source cards above.

Important Note

Landor's Curve and PathDecider provide directional career analysis, planning tools, and founder-developed frameworks based on public workforce data, published research, and AI-assisted comparison. They are not guarantees of employment, official labor forecasts, or substitutes for professional, financial, legal, educational, or career counseling. The goal is to help users make more informed decisions, not to tell them what they must do.
