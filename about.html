What This Is and What It Is Not
Directional Tool — Not a Forecast

The Durability Scores, displacement assessments, opportunity zones, and career recommendations on this site are directional estimates drawn from publicly available workforce research. They are not predictions of individual outcomes. No research source, including the ones used here, can tell you exactly what will happen to your specific career in your specific market. Use this site to ask better questions, see patterns you might have missed, and start a conversation. Do not use it as a guarantee.

Full Disclosure
Where the Data Comes From

Every analysis on this site draws from the following publicly available research sources. Each one has real strengths and real limitations. Both are listed honestly.

O*NET
Occupational Information Network — US Dept. of Labor
Last updated: Rolling updates, 2024–2025
The most granular job-level dataset available. Breaks down the specific tasks, skills, knowledge, and work activities that make up each occupation.
Strength
Extremely detailed task-level data. Closest thing to an x-ray of what a job actually requires day to day.
Limitation
Updated on a rolling cycle. Very new and rapidly evolving roles may lag behind reality.
BLS
Bureau of Labor Statistics — US Dept. of Labor
Last updated: 2024–2025 Occupational Outlook Handbook
The official US government source for employment data, wage statistics, and ten-year job outlook projections. The most cited source in labor market research.
Strength
Authoritative and widely trusted. The gold standard for employment numbers and long-range projections.
Limitation
Conservative by design. Measures what has already happened. Often underestimates the speed of disruption.
Brookings
Brookings Institution
Last updated: 2023–2024 automation and AI research
An independent policy research organization that produces widely respected research on automation exposure, wage polarization, and workforce transitions.
Strength
Strong on automation risk and displacement analysis. One of the most cited independent sources on AI and the future of work.
Limitation
Research-focused. Not always translated into practical individual career guidance.
McKinsey
McKinsey Global Institute
Last updated: 2024 The State of AI report
The research arm of McKinsey and Company. Produces major annual reports on automation, workforce transitions, and the economics of AI adoption globally.
Strength
Deep, well-resourced research with global scope. Strong economic modeling of disruption at scale.
Limitation
Produced by a consulting firm with clients in affected industries. Read with that context in mind.
Goldman Sachs
Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research
Last updated: 2023–2024 AI and labor market reports
Economic research from one of the world's largest investment banks on automation, AI displacement, and labor market transformation.
Strength
Strong quantitative modeling. Particularly useful on which job categories are exposed to generative AI.
Limitation
Written for investors, not workers. The framing can differ from what is useful for individual career decisions.
WEF
World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs Report
Last updated: Future of Jobs Report 2025
An annual report from the WEF surveying employers globally on which roles are growing, shrinking, and transforming. One of the few sources with true international scope.
Strength
International reach. Captures global trends beyond US-centric data. Forward-looking by design.
Limitation
Based on employer surveys. Reflects what employers believe will happen, not necessarily what will.
LinkedIn
LinkedIn Workforce Confidence Reports and Jobs on the Rise
Last updated: Quarterly — publicly available reports
Publicly available quarterly and annual reports from LinkedIn based on real hiring trends, skill demand, and workforce movement across their platform.
Strength
Real hiring signal. Shows what employers are actually posting and paying for right now, not projections.
Limitation
Skews toward white-collar and professional fields. Less representative of trades, healthcare, and blue-collar work.
OECD
OECD Employment Outlook
Last updated: OECD Employment Outlook 2024
Annual workforce analysis from the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development covering 38 member countries. Focused on automation, inequality, and labor market transitions.
Strength
Rigorous, independent, internationally comparative. Strong on policy implications of workforce disruption.
Limitation
Policy-focused. Can feel abstract relative to on-the-ground career decisions.
