Transparency

OUR SOURCES
& TRANSPARENCY

Trust is everything. Landor's Curve is built to show where its information comes from, how that information is used, and what users should understand before making decisions.

Section 1
Why This Page Exists

Better career insight should not be reserved only for institutions with large budgets. Landor's Curve brings together public workforce data, published research, current signals, user-provided information, and structured analysis so individuals can ask better questions about work, education, risk, opportunity, and career change.

The goal is not to overwhelm users with raw data. The goal is to make complex information easier to understand, compare, and apply.

Section 2
The Landor's Curve Analysis Engine

Landor's Curve does not rely on one source or one type of analysis. The system combines several layers of information to generate directional career insights.

Those layers may include official workforce data, published labor-market research, occupational and education data, user-provided resumes or job descriptions, current job-market signals when available, founder-developed frameworks, and AI-assisted synthesis.

Section 3
Source Layers

Each tool draws from different combinations of these layers depending on the type of analysis being performed.

Layer 1
Official Workforce Data
Used for occupational tasks, skills, wages, employment outlook, training requirements, and labor-market baselines.
O*NET BLS Public occupational databases
Official data provides the baseline. When new official data becomes available, Landor's Curve is designed to update its underlying references so users are not relying on outdated assumptions.
Layer 2
Published Research and Reports
Used for broader workforce trends, AI exposure, automation pressure, industry shifts, education trends, and long-term labor-market interpretation.
Brookings McKinsey Goldman Sachs WEF OECD
Published reports help capture larger patterns that may not appear in occupational databases right away. These reports are reviewed and incorporated when they add useful context.
Layer 3
Current Signals
Used for recent job-market patterns, employer language, emerging skill demand, role descriptions, and recently published material. Some tools may use current signals when available, especially for deeper reports or more specific career comparisons. These signals are not meant to replace official data. They help show what may be changing now.
Landor's Curve is being built to support curated current-signal layers. The goal is not to scrape everything blindly. The goal is to add useful, human-reviewed signals that improve the quality of the analysis.
Layer 4
User Inputs
User inputs help the tools personalize results. A resume, job description, or career goal can change the analysis because the system is comparing general workforce information against the user's actual background.
Resume uploads Career background Job descriptions Target roles Study hours Education goals
Layer 5
Founder-Developed Frameworks
Landor's Curve also uses founder-developed frameworks to organize the analysis. These frameworks are designed to help users understand how careers bend, shift, or transfer as technology and market expectations change. They are used for interpreting career pressure, skill transfer, adjacent opportunities, learning paths, and adaptation. They are working frameworks, not peer-reviewed academic studies.
Section 4
How AI Is Used
What AI Does in This System

Landor's Curve uses AI as part of a structured analysis process. The system compares source material, user-provided inputs, job descriptions, reports, and career data to identify patterns, surface contradictions, organize options, and generate plain-language explanations.

The value is speed, structure, and comparison. The Landor's Curve analysis process can compare more information more quickly than manual research alone would allow, helping users see patterns, options, and questions they may not have noticed yet.

The system uses available sources and user-provided input to generate reports, roadmaps, resume analysis, and career recommendations. It is tuned for source-grounded analysis, consistency, and practical comparison rather than creative guessing. We continue to review and improve the process so the tools stay focused on useful, traceable, and reliable outputs.

To increase the value of these findings, we encourage users to bring them into conversations with counselors, advisors, mentors, educators, employers, or other qualified professionals. The output is meant to support better research, better questions, and better conversations. It is an assistant for planning, not a replacement for human judgment or professional guidance when that guidance is needed.

Section 5
Updates and Currency
How Fresh Is the Data?

Landor's Curve uses a combination of stable public data, manually reviewed research, current signals, and user-provided information.

When new official data becomes available, the goal is to update the system as quickly as practical. When new reports or useful current signals appear, they may be reviewed and added when they improve the quality of the analysis.

Some information is stable and changes slowly. Some information changes quickly. Landor's Curve is being built to make those differences clearer over time.

As the platform grows, the goal is to give users more control over source layers. For example, users may eventually be able to compare results using official data only, recently published research, current market signals, education-path data, or curated human-reviewed signal layers.

Section 6
What This Website Is
What It Is

Landor's Curve is a career decision-support tool. It brings together public workforce data, published research, current signals, user-provided information, and structured analysis to help people understand their options more clearly.

It is designed to assist, not dictate. The goal is to help users compare paths, identify risks, notice transferable skills, and ask better questions before making important career, education, or employment decisions.

What It Is Not

It is not a guarantee of employment, an official labor forecast, or a replacement for professional career, financial, legal, or educational advice.

It is not affiliated with or sponsored by any school, training program, employer, or institution trying to steer users toward a specific outcome.

Section 7

Landor's Curve is built to be independent and transparent. We are not sponsored by schools, employers, training programs, or placement services trying to steer users toward a specific outcome. Recommendations are based on the information available, the user's inputs, and the structure of the analysis, not on who paid to be recommended.

Section 8
Using the Results
How to Get the Most Out of This Tool

Landor's Curve is designed to support career research, planning, and conversation. The results can help users organize their thinking, compare possible paths, and prepare better questions for counselors, advisors, mentors, educators, employers, or other professionals.

For major decisions involving education, finances, employment transitions, or legal matters, users should consider the results alongside other trusted sources and qualified guidance.
